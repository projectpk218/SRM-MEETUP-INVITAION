Research-Backed Prompt Specification for a Senior Alumni-Meet Digital Invitation
Executive Summary
The strongest alumni invitation should not look like a generic college fest poster. For senior alumni, the invitation has to communicate three things almost instantly: institutional credibility, emotional reconnection, and the practical reason to attend. Current reunion programs from INSEAD, MIT Sloan, and the University at Buffalo consistently frame alumni gatherings around reconnecting with classmates, returning to the institution, learning from faculty or leaders, networking, and celebrating shared history; senior-alumni programs also explicitly surface practical details such as dress code, registration, agenda, and contacts. 

The recommended system therefore treats the coined event name as the emotional brand, while the institution, date, venue, and RSVP provide immediate trust and usability. The invitation should be available in three selectable voices: Premium/Formal, Warm-Casual, and Modern-MBA. These are not three completely different brands; they are three expressions of one alumni identity.

Accessibility matters particularly for a senior audience. WCAG 2.2 calls for at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text, while W3C's work on older users specifically highlights reduced contrast sensitivity and the value of readable text and sufficiently large controls. 
 HTML email should preserve important information as real text rather than embedding everything in a poster image, and a practical CTA target of about 44×44 pixels or larger is appropriate for touch use; Mailchimp explicitly recommends that size for email CTAs. 

The recommended production approach is one responsive master design system adapted to channel-specific formats: approximately 660 px content width for email, 1080×1350 for portrait social/direct messaging, 1080×1920 for Stories/vertical status, and an A5 portrait invitation PDF with an optional A4 version. Mailchimp's current builder uses a 660 px email width and recommends 660–1320 px images, while Canva's current size guide lists 1080×1350 for portrait Instagram images and 1080×1920 for Stories. 

What remains unspecified
No final coined event brand has been selected in the conversation, so the prompt uses [EVENT_BRAND] rather than inventing another name. The institution, date, time, venue, virtual link, RSVP details, dress code, speakers, hosts, agenda, contacts, social handles, hashtag, logo, and imagery are also unspecified and remain explicit placeholders.

The final prompt at the end of this report is designed so those fields can be supplied later without rewriting the creative brief.

Research Basis and Event Strategy
What premium alumni programs signal
A useful benchmark is not a commercial party invitation but official university reunion communication. INSEAD's October 2026 MBA reunion for alumni 35–50 years after graduation combines emotional language about returning, rekindling friendships, and reconnecting with the school with a substantive program of dean/faculty sessions, class meetings, networking, and meals. It also clearly states practical information such as business-casual campus attire, formal-dinner attire, registration information, and contacts. 

MIT Sloan's 2026 reunion similarly emphasizes three dimensions: reconnecting with classmates, learning from faculty, and developing new friendships and professional relationships. 
 University at Buffalo's global reunion illustrates the more ceremonial end of the spectrum, combining alumni connection with a keynote, institutional legacy, dinner, and formal dress. 

That leads to a useful strategic formula for this invitation:

Shared origin + present achievement + renewed connection + future relevance

For senior alumni, nostalgia should therefore be present but not dominate. The design should say, in effect: we remember where we began, we respect what everyone has become, and there is value in gathering again.

Tone, Copy, and Brand Language
The following editorial limits are design recommendations derived from the need to keep social/mobile invitations scannable; they are not external standards.

Tone	Voice	Recommended copy limits	Sample headline
Premium / Formal	Distinguished, restrained, gracious, ceremonial; emphasizes legacy and stature	Headline 3–7 words; hook 8–16; description 25–45; CTA 2–5; reminder 15–30	A Distinguished Evening of Return
Warm-Casual	Personal, nostalgic, welcoming, conversational without becoming youthful or slang-heavy	Headline 3–6 words; hook 8–18; description 25–50; CTA 2–5; reminder 15–35	Back Where It Began
Modern-MBA	Crisp, intelligent, confident, network-oriented and future-facing	Headline 2–5 words; hook 7–14; description 20–40; CTA 2–4; reminder 12–28	Reconnect. Reflect. Reimagine.

The Modern-MBA direction is particularly well aligned with business-school reunion programming because schools such as MIT Sloan and INSEAD combine reunion with faculty learning, leadership discussion, and networking rather than treating the occasion as purely social.

Visual System, Formats, and Accessibility
Visual directions
The institution's own brand system should override every generic palette or type recommendation below. Major university identity systems such as MIT's and Stanford's explicitly specify logo clear space, approved use, and placement constraints; that is evidence for following the actual institution's identity rules rather than improvising around the university mark. 

Tone	Suggested palette	Typography direction	Imagery
Premium / Formal	Midnight #0B1F33, ivory #F7F2E8, charcoal #1F2933, muted gold #B08D57 as accent only	Refined serif display + highly legible sans-serif body	Architectural campus details, archival/current campus pairing, elegant alumni portraits, restrained linework
Warm-Casual	Forest #173A2B, cream #FFF8EB, terracotta #A64B2A, cocoa #5B4636	Warm serif or humanist sans with friendly spacing	Authentic alumni moments, campus landmarks, subtle archival photographs, memory-card treatment
Modern-MBA	Deep cobalt #193B7A, off-white #F8FAFC, teal #0F766E, graphite #334155	Clean geometric/humanist sans; optional restrained serif accent	Executive/network imagery, architectural grids, abstract connection lines, faculty/alumni speaker imagery

The muted gold in the formal palette should be treated as an accent rather than small body text. Every final foreground/background pair must be tested, because WCAG AA requires at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text. 

For senior alumni, favor readable mixed-case typography, generous spacing, restrained line lengths, and clear controls. W3C notes that aging can involve reduced contrast sensitivity and changes in visual perception, and its older-user guidance emphasizes readable text, contrast, understandable presentation, and large links/buttons. 

Logo and event-brand placement
Use the official uploaded institution logo, never a generated approximation. Do not distort, redraw, crowd, or invent a new university lock-up. Preserve whatever clear space and minimum size the institution's own identity guide specifies. MIT, for example, explicitly places its logo in digital newsletter headers, while Stanford specifies clear space around its marks; those are examples of the broader principle, not rules to copy numerically for another institution. 

Recommended hierarchy:

Institution logo → “presents” or alumni-office descriptor → coined [EVENT_BRAND] → tagline

The coined event mark can be more expressive than the institution logo, but it should remain visually subordinate enough that recipients immediately understand the invitation is authentic.

Accessibility requirements
The template should enforce these as acceptance criteria, not optional polish:

All ordinary text/background combinations meet WCAG 2.2 AA 4.5:1; large text meets at least 3:1. Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning. 
HTML email keeps the event name, date, location, RSVP information, and essential message as real HTML text, not only inside a raster graphic. Mailchimp specifically recommends keeping important messages in HTML and using semantic headings for accessible email. 
Use a comfortably large RSVP button; 44×44 px or larger is a practical email target recommendation. WCAG 2.2 itself establishes a 24×24 CSS-pixel minimum target subject to defined exceptions, while its enhanced guidance encourages larger targets. 
Informative images receive concise, meaningful alt text. Decorative graphics receive empty/null alt treatment where supported. Mailchimp recommends short descriptive alt text, while W3C accessibility guidance distinguishes meaningful from decorative imagery. 
Do not put crucial information exclusively in low-contrast gold, thin type, italics, decorative scripts, or an image overlay.
Digital PDF output should preserve selectable text, clickable URLs, meaningful reading order, and image descriptions when the connector/export pipeline supports tagged PDFs.

==================================================
ACCESSIBILITY — REQUIRED
==================================================

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast targets:
- normal text: at least 4.5:1
- qualifying large text: at least 3:1

Do not rely on color alone to communicate information.

For senior readability:
- favor clear mixed-case typography
- avoid long all-capital passages
- avoid thin low-contrast text
- avoid long italic passages
- use generous spacing
- keep essential details visually large and obvious
- avoid long centered body paragraphs

For HTML:
- keep event name, date, venue, description, dress code, agenda, contact, and RSVP as real HTML text
- do NOT flatten essential information into one poster image
- use semantic headings
- use descriptive link/button labels
- create a comfortably sized CTA target, preferably at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels
- ensure keyboard-visible links/buttons where supported

For images:
Create concise ALT TEXT.
Informative image alt text must explain its purpose/content.
Decorative images should use empty/null alt treatment where supported.
Do not repeat surrounding text unnecessarily.

Create an ALT-TEXT MANIFEST containing:
asset name | purpose | recommended alt text | decorative yes/no.

For PDF:
Where supported, retain selectable text, meaningful reading order, tags/headings, descriptive links, and image descriptions.
